# Atmospheric scoop divers v1.0.1 — design and publication reference

Published article: [Actively Cooled Atmospheric Scoop Diver Concept](https://medium.com/@alanse/actively-cooled-atmospheric-scoop-diver-concept-8ae047bc5bb7).

**A 317 m orbital factory that stores dive heat in solid coolers, then unfolds
those coolers in space.** The accepted numerical reference produces **3.086 tonnes
of useful new cryogenic liquid per 106-minute orbit**, with **306.4 days** of
operating-orbit production equaling its **12,840-tonne dry mass**.

Version **1.0.1** is finalized as a numerical design, not flight-qualified hardware.
Its source checkpoint is `5a33ef6`; [exact inputs](../../configs/transient_v1_0_1_valve.json)
and the refined trajectory remain frozen. This is the main version document,
consolidating the publication material and the completed valve-reserve restoration.
Temporary implementation steps and superseded algebraic starting estimates are
not current design requirements. See the [version index](README.md).

## Architecture and operating sequence

Five finite gas/cooler volumes and four dive compressors connect the open inlet
to the main receiver. The first cooler is SiC; the remaining four are aluminum.
The fifth cooler lies **inside the main receiver**, densifying gas during filling.
It is not a fifth dive compressor or a cooler in the thruster reserve.

1. **Approach:** the isolated reserve supplies gaseous propellant for battery-backed
   finite-duration reboost. Stow deployable equipment for the atmospheric pass.
2. **Collect:** ram-supported entry, gas-to-solid cooling and compression fill the
   main receiver. The inlet has no door. Thrusters continue running.
3. **Share feed:** when main-receiver pressure catches reserve pressure, open the
   finite-flow connection. Gas can move in either direction; the reserve refills
   while feeding thrust. It isolates after the burn, before coast product drawdown.
4. **Process and recover:** coast compressors and the liquefaction train draw from
   the main receiver into liquid tanks. Propellant bypasses liquefaction. The
   pressurized reserve stays isolated. Empty front chambers release their racks
   at +12 minutes; receiver rack release is scheduled at +72 minutes. Solar and
   warm radiators deploy; the solid racks radiate their stored heat.

The cold plate inventories absorb a short heat pulse rather than requiring all
heat rejection at the hottest instant. Four staggered rows use wider physical
channels and a smaller *effective* thermal spacing in the reduced heat-transfer
model. Friction is charged to compressor electricity without a resolved hydraulic
pressure field. The final flat rack-temperature tails are controlled cooldown
targets in the model, not passive equilibrium temperatures.

## Physical layout and reserve

The front blocks occupy x=0–48.097 m. The main receiver spans **48.097–192 m**, with
**25.901 m barrel diameter** after package displacement. The full-bore reserve
spans **192–317 m**, diameter **30 m**. Two rounded heads per tank are included.
External aft thrusters do not displace internal gas volume. Radial equipment sectors
remain illustrative. The updated stowed mass allocation estimates dry CM at
**118.5 m from the mouth (37.4% of hull length)**. This is a packaging estimate,
not an aerodynamic stability result. See the [axial mass register](../../investigations/publication_v1_0_1/AXIAL_MASS.md)
for placements and the proportional-margin assumption.

The 125 m reserve is a fixed choice **in this version**, not a future project-level
requirement. It starts with **3,513.831 kg** and feeds **1,441.186 kg** before the
valve opens at **−43.616 s**, when both sides are **1.899 kPa**. Over the full burn,
**3,101.412 kg** is consumed and **3,101.402 kg** passes into the reserve net. Its
minimum inventory is **2,072.302 kg**. The cycle closes within 0.010 kg.
[The refill graph and audit](../../investigations/publication_v1_0_1/README.md)
show that fill-as-you-use works; reserve sizing was not optimized.

There is **no reserve refill compressor or boosted storage bank**. Finite valve
area is 4 m²; the modeled feed passage is 1 m² discharging to 100 Pa. Its minimum
capacity of 3.68 kg/s exceeds 2.51 kg/s burn demand. Qualification of real hardware
at these pressures remains open.

## Conceptual propulsion versus collection split

The [subsystem payoff investigation](../../investigations/subsystem_payoff/README.md)
allocates shared arrays, batteries, bus and thermal hardware without double counting.
At the same net-liquid production rate, propulsion accounts for **235.04 days**
and collection/processing **71.36 days**. They sum to 306.40 days. These are
contributions to joint-system payoff, not independently operating spacecraft.

A separate paper calculation spreads the same impulse over the full orbit,
removes the allocated propulsion battery, and scales thruster mass by duty factor.
It gives **138.66 days** for the modified propulsion allocation. Other allocated
hardware is retained. This is not a physically solved continuous-thrust mission,
and does not change the version's payoff or delivery boundary.

## How the model produces a design

The calculation starts with a proposed spacecraft and follows it through one
orbit. It asks how much new liquid that installed hardware produces while
returning its working inventories and stored energy to their starting states.
An outer constrained optimizer changes the proposal and tries again.

1. **Choose the architecture and the scope locks.** For v1.0.1 these include the
   31.7 m outside diameter, 317 m length, four dive compressors, five cooling
   volumes, SiC followed by aluminum, open mouth, valve-only reserve and 2,000 km
   apogee. The 125 m reserve length is fixed for this version. Material properties,
   equipment mass relationships, efficiencies and deployment speed are technology
   assumptions, not quantities the optimizer can improve for free.

2. **Propose the adjustable numbers.** A candidate supplies 46 scalar values:
   rack inventories, sheet thicknesses and effective gaps; initial rack
   temperatures; compressor capabilities and motor ratings; solar, battery and
   thermal-system sizes; liquefier capacity; thruster Isp and burn duration;
   perigee; and initial reserve inventory and temperature. Initial conditions
   are part of the design problem because they must be recovered by the next
   orbit. The [coordinate register](../../modeling/optimization/VALVE_COORDINATES.md)
   gives the actual values and bounds.

3. **Build the installed spacecraft on paper, before flying it.** Rack geometry
   determines solid volume, gas-contact area and minimum chamber size. Compressor
   capabilities determine machinery size and mass. Folded arrays, radiators,
   batteries and processing equipment consume space around the receiver. These
   allocations determine the remaining gas volume within the fixed hull.
   Pressure ratings size the vessel shells; they do not prescribe the gas's
   operating pressure. Component mass relationships plus the 25% margin produce
   dry mass. Equipment does not silently grow during the simulated pass to meet
   whatever loads arise.

4. **Calculate the encounter and the required reboost.** The chosen ellipse gives
   altitude and speed versus time. The atmosphere model supplies density, from
   which the model obtains incident mass supply, inlet support pressure and the
   momentum burden. The finite-burn calculation supplies the impulse penalty for
   the selected burn duration. Isp and propulsion efficiency turn that requirement
   into propellant, thrust and electrical demand. This is prescribed orbital
   forcing with a reboost balance, not a trajectory freely integrated under drag
   and thrust, and not a resolved inlet shock simulation.

5. **Integrate the collection pass with finite hardware.** Each chamber tracks
   species mass and gas energy; each rack tracks stored heat. Inflow, outflow,
   compression and gas-to-solid heat transfer change those states over time.
   Pressure follows from inventory, temperature and available volume—it is not
   an independently imposed history. Compressor work uses real-species
   isentropic enthalpy changes with an efficiency penalty, while chamber states
   use the reduced mixture-property treatment. Rack resistance adds compressor
   electrical cost in this version; it does not directly solve the hydraulic
   flow restriction. The reserve initially supplies thrust, then its valve opens
   when receiver pressure catches up, allowing collection to replenish it.

6. **Continue through coast and recovery.** After reboost, the reserve isolates.
   The main receiver supplies coast compression and liquefaction. Product enters
   liquid tanks; the receiver's mass and pressure fall. Racks deploy on their
   scheduled release, and their radiation changes as they cool. Solar production,
   radiator rejection, deployment time, battery charging and the waste-heat
   buffer are tracked through the cycle. Rack 5 remains inside the receiver
   longer than the upstream racks. These time histories establish whether the
   hardware can handle the pulse and recover afterward, not merely whether an
   orbit-averaged energy budget balances.

7. **Check repeatability and constraints.** At the end, compare starting and
   ending rack enthalpies, battery energy, waste-heat-buffer energy, residual gas
   and rotor energy, and reserve species inventory and energy. Their closure
   residuals must lie within specified tolerances; useful new product is allowed
   to accumulate. Also check material temperatures, vessel pressures, electrical
   availability, thrust delivery, tank capacity, feed availability and collection
   completeness. The receiver peak must match its specified working-pressure
   target within 50 Pa. Perigee therefore participates in matching atmospheric
   supply to this craft's capacity; it is not simply held at the old 85 km value.

8. **Calculate payoff, change the candidate, and verify the selected result.**
   For a feasible repeating cycle, operating-orbit mass payoff is

   \[
   t_{\rm payoff}=\frac{M_{\rm dry}}{\Delta m_{\rm useful\ liquid}}\,T_{\rm orbit}.
   \]

   The denominator is new useful liquid after propellant consumption, losses and
   restoration of working inventory; recycled coolant or initial inventory is
   not production. COBYLA adjusts the candidate to reduce this payoff while
   satisfying the constraints. Each candidate gets **one forward cycle**, not an
   internal loop that repeatedly flies it until it settles. The optimizer solves
   performance and closure together. Finally, the selected inputs are replayed
   with tighter integration settings and conservation checks before figures and
   tables are generated.

For the accepted replay, **12,840.22 tonnes dry**, **3.0859 tonnes of new liquid
per orbit**, and a **106.04-minute orbit** give **306.40 days**. This is an
operating-orbit result: circular-apogee export and delivery remain unimplemented.
Numerical closure establishes consistency within the chosen component models;
it does not validate reacting inlet flow, reusable leading-edge life, detailed
compressor maps or deployment mechanics. The planned staged worksheet/subsystem
method is future development, not how this frozen result was obtained.

Fixed: diameter, L/D=10, 125 m reserve, 2,000 km apogee, stage/material choices,
4.767 kPa receiver target, pressure ratings, deployment and feed capability
assumptions. Package displacement determines main-receiver volume; its gas
pressure and temperature evolve. Volume, pressure and temperature are not three
independent freely chosen fill states.


Perigee was searched over 80–100 km, while receiver peak was required within
50 Pa of the fixed **4,766.83 Pa working target**. The **6,500 Pa receiver/reserve
rating** is a separate structural ceiling. Initial reserve mass/temperature
were outer closure coordinates. Bus and battery discharge ratings derive from
installed power demands before the forward integration; package volumes are
charged once. All modeled collection must be admitted within the declared 0.1%
allowance rather than rewarding an undersized inlet by discarding supply.

COBYLA terminated successfully after 284 objective evaluations; initialization
probes explain the larger log count. An independent tighter-resolution replay
returns 306.398724 days versus 306.400559 days at search resolution. It satisfies
species/energy closure, thermal/material/pressure limits, full thrust and
**99.9787% capture of modeled incident supply**. This is a local scoped result,
not a global optimum or qualification of an 85.8 km reusable leading edge.

The **275.73-day boosted-reserve design was rejected** and is [archived](../design_archive/v1.0.1_rejected_boosted/README.md).
Compared with it, dry mass fell 3.09% but liquid per orbit fell 12.78%, giving
30.67 more payoff days. Several assumptions changed together; this is not a
controlled measurement of the reserve's isolated penalty. Compared with the
original 278.02-day snapshot, the increase is 28.38 days.

## What is inherited and what this study adds

The [published literature review](https://medium.com/@alanse/atmosphere-orbital-scoop-diver-literature-db3aba700a28)
and [source comparison](LITERATURE_COMPARISON.md) distinguish Minovitch's 1988
patent, Slough's 2022 RON and the later Space Gas Station concept. Atmospheric
collection, staged compression, coast processing and elliptical perigee reboost
have precedents. This study examines a specific solid-storage/deployment cycle,
physical packing, finite equipment sizing and periodic energy/inventory closure.
It does not establish historical priority or patent novelty.

## Version boundary

The [v1.0.0-alpha account](V1_0_0_ALPHA.md) records the original LN₂-mixing and door-retention
idea. Solid racks cool without adding gas inventory—the key storage advantage
behind this version. The alpha account also explains why inlet chemistry could
justify revisiting limited mixing. The [historical v1.0.0 kit](V1_0_0_ARCHIVE.md)
retains the later original numerical snapshot. Development proposals, including
staged sizing and internally sized reserve geometry, are consolidated in the
[issue-review index](../../local-issue-drafts/README.md). v1.1 [fixed solid thermal stores](../V1_1_FIXED_THERMAL_STORES.md)
with coast coolant recovery and v2.0 mothership/tether operation remain future
versions. No future improvement is credited to v1.0.1.
